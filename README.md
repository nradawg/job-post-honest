**Use this when** you are posting a role and would rather screen the wrong
people out now than find out in week three.

## The rule

> Everything you leave out of the post shows up in week three.
> Pay, schedule, and the worst part of the job go in.

Candidates already decode the standard vagueness. Hiding the hard parts does
not widen your pool, it fills it with people who will leave when they find out,
and you pay for that twice.

## How it works

**1. I check whether you are required to post a range.** Several US states and
a growing list of cities require a pay range in the posting itself. The list
changes most years, the vendor summaries contradict each other on both the
count and the thresholds, and I will not guess your current rule. Lookup: your
state labor office at https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/contacts, plus
your city if it has its own ordinance.

**2. I never invent your range.** A number goes in only if you gave it to me
and can say where it came from. Otherwise the line reads `GUESSED` and I hand
you the lookup. A post with a made up range is a post you have to walk back on
the first call.

**3. I write the quit list before the post.** Three things about this specific
job that people leave over. If I do not know them, I guess out loud and label
it, because a quit list I invented describes a job that is not yours.

**4. I put the quit list in the post** in plain words, stated as fact, not
spun into a perk. The reader decides. That is the whole mechanism.

**5. I state the real schedule.** Days, hours, earliest start, on call,
weekends, travel, who covers holidays.

## The decoder

| what you want to write | what they read | write this instead |
|---|---|---|
| competitive salary | below market | the range, or that you state it on call one |
| fast paced | understaffed | "two people cover this now, you are the third" |
| wear many hats | three jobs | the list of jobs |
| like a family here | no boundaries | the actual hours |
| rockstar, ninja | underpaid and flattered | the title |
| flexible hours | always reachable | the hours you need covered |
| must be a self starter | nobody will train you | who trains them, for how long |

```
GRILLING MYSELF ON: post for part time ops coordinator

A  Unknown, and it is not a thing I can settle from here.
   GUESSED nothing. Lookup handed back, dol.gov state labor offices.
   If yes, the post cannot ship without it.

Q  What is the range?
A  Not given.
   GUESSED nothing. I will not fill this in.
   Lookup: what you paid the last person, plus two live local postings
   for the same title.

A  Mon to Fri, phones live by 7am, one Saturday morning a month.
   FROM YOU, you said the crew rolls out at 7.

Q  Top three quit reasons for this job?

   2 Angry customer calls land here first, before you.
   3 It is the only administrative seat, so there is no backup when
     they take a day.
   GUESSED all three, from the role shape. Correct these, they are
   the part of the post that does the work.

Q  Is there a career path to name?
A  No evidence of one.
   GUESSED. Saying "growth opportunity" with nothing behind it is the
   line people quote back at you in month four.

5 answers. 3 guessed, 2 lookups handed back.
Fix the range and the quit list, then I write the post.
```

## Limits

The trap: honesty theater. A post that is candid about the coffee and vague
about the money reads worse than a plain one, because the candor makes the
omission obvious. If the range is missing, the post says it is missing and why.

Second trap: posting a range you cannot actually pay to look compliant. That
converts into a withdrawn offer, which is more expensive than a narrow range
honestly stated.

This is not legal review. Posting requirements, protected class language, and
independent contractor classification all vary by jurisdiction and change, so
anything load bearing goes past someone who does this for a living.

It cannot tell you whether your pay is competitive, and it cannot make a bad
job attractive. An honest post about a job nobody wants gets you fewer
applicants, correctly.

## How this one knows what it knows

Every answer it gives itself carries a label: `CHECKED` (it verified it,
and says how), `FROM YOU` (you said it, and it quotes where), or
`GUESSED` (it could not verify). Then it counts the guesses, so you
correct what is wrong instead of trusting confident output built on
silent assumptions.

> Never ask for something you can check.
> Never assume something you cannot check.
